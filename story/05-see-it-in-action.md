# See It in Action 👀

### Less talking about Sonny. More showing what he actually helped with.

I could tell you all about workflows and knowledge files. But it's easier to show you what a consultant would see.

The examples below are **illustrative reconstructions using a fictional customer** — not screenshots of live company systems or verbatim outputs. They show the structure and guardrails I used, without exposing customer information.

---

## 01 / After a customer meeting

The original problem: a call ends, and the real admin work begins. I used Sonny to turn meeting summaries into a recap I could review and send.

![Example customer recap drafted by Sonny](assets/01-meeting-recap.png)

**What mattered:** clear decisions, assigned next steps, and missing information visibly marked as `[pending]`. Sonny didn't send the email; I reviewed it first.

<details>
<summary><b>What I checked before sending</b></summary>

- Did the recap match what was actually discussed?
- Were action owners and dates supported by the notes?
- Were missing details flagged rather than guessed?
- Was the language clear enough for the customer?

</details>

---

## 02 / When a support issue came up

I didn't want Support to spend another round asking for information we already had. Sonny prepared a consistent ticket draft with the basics in one place.

![Example structured support ticket](assets/02-support-ticket.png)

**What mattered:** customer, issue, affected users, impact, requested action, and urgency. Sonny was also instructed to check for an existing open ticket before suggesting another.

<details>
<summary><b>The boundary</b></summary>

A draft wasn't a diagnosis. If the cause wasn't confirmed, Sonny couldn't pretend it was. A consultant still checked the facts and decided whether to submit the ticket.

</details>

---

## 03 / When a consultant needed a second opinion

The team copilot was built to make implementation knowledge easier to access. One important use case was helping consultants think through scope questions without making promises on the company's behalf.

![Example consultant copilot response](assets/03-consultant-copilot.png)

**What mattered:** a useful next step, grounded in the agreed scope. Not an invented product capability or an automatic approval.

This reflects the **copilot I built and began piloting**. It isn't evidence of a measured team-wide rollout.

---

## The common thread

All three examples follow the same idea:

**Gather context → prepare something useful → flag uncertainty → let a person decide.**

In my own recap and ticket workflow, preparation dropped from roughly **30–60 minutes to under five minutes**. That's an observed personal result, not a controlled study.

The part I wanted to test next was whether the same quality and time savings held up for other consultants — and whether that translated into a better customer experience.
