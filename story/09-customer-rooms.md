# 🏠 The Big Idea: A Room for Every Customer

### Where implementation meets the sales experience.

---

*An idea, not built.*

## Where it started

Every recap Sonny wrote pointed the customer to the same place: their own resource page, with recordings and materials from the project.

That page was already a mini Digital Sales Room for after the sale. But it didn't know anything Sonny knew about the project, like the customer's goals, open questions or risks.

I've seen this from a few angles: as a customer running an enablement platform, as a Sales Engineer building custom demos, and as an implementation leader working with Digital Sales Rooms. It's always the same story. **Buying feels personal. Then implementation starts with a generic welcome email.**

## The idea

Whatever Sonny knows from the contract, the kickoff check and every recap should build the room: why the customer bought, what success looks like, the plan, every session, and what we still need from them. All in one place, and all personal to them.

## The part I'm most excited about

Sales teams already watch how buyers use a sales room. After the sale, those same signals say a lot about how the project is going. If the executive sponsor never opens the room, nobody's really steering. If it gets forwarded to IT the week before login setup, that's a great sign.

Feed that back into the kickoff check, and it stops being a one-time snapshot. It becomes something that keeps updating for the whole project.

## Keeping it safe

Everything in the room is customer-facing, so **a consultant approves everything before customers see it.** Sonny builds, a person publishes.

## Why it's third, not first

The room is the shiniest piece and the easiest to demo. That's exactly why it shouldn't come first. A beautiful room built on a contract nobody checked just presents the wrong plan more convincingly.
